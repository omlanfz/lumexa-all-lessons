# Lesson 8: Showcase — Presenting Your AI Project to the World

## Big Idea
Sharing what you built helps other people learn, and helps YOU explain your thinking like a real engineer!

## Learning Objectives
- Choose one of the 3 AI portfolio projects (Rock-Paper-Scissors, Voice Story, or Emotion Pet) to present.
- Explain, in their own words, the Examples → Training → Model → Prediction → Action pipeline using their own project as the example.
- Practice presenting clearly: what the project does, what stands in for AI today, and what a real trained AI model would add.

## Teacher Preparation
- No new website account required.
- Optional: if any student has already completed the real AI upgrade (webcam gesture/voice/expression recognition) by following the integration guides, let them demo that live — otherwise, presenting the complete stand-in version is equally valid and expected.
- Set up a simple presentation space (a chair/table with a laptop, or just gather round).
- Prepare a simple presentation checklist/rubric to display (see Guided Activity).

## Required Files
- Any of the 3 `Projects/Project_0N_*/project.sb3` files and their guides.

## Teaching Flow (approx. 50 minutes)
1. Warm-up / Hook — 5 min
2. Concept Introduction (recap the whole course) — 5 min
3. Teacher Demonstration (model a short presentation) — 5 min
4. Guided Activity (practice in pairs) — 15 min
5. Main Activity (showcase presentations) — 15 min
6. Recap + Celebration — 5 min

## Warm-up / Hook
🚀 **Coding Mission:** "Today you're not coding — you're being an AI SCIENTIST explaining your work! Real AI engineers present their projects to teams all the time."

## Concept Introduction
Recap the whole course quickly: "We started by learning AI is patterns from examples, not magic. We trained real image and sound models. We connected AI to Scratch. And we built three complete games/stories/pets that are ready for a real AI upgrade." Emphasize: presenting your reasoning is as important as the code itself.

🧠 **Think Like a Coder:** "A great presentation always says: what it does, how it works, and what's next — that's exactly the structure of good documentation real engineers write."

## Teacher Demonstration
Model a 60-90 second sample presentation about one project, hitting exactly 3 points:
1. "This is my [project name]. Here's what it does..." (demo the game/story/pet)
2. "Right now, [keypress/click] stands in for the AI. Here's what would replace it..." (name the exact ML4Kids/Teachable Machine block)
3. "The AI idea behind this is Examples → Training → Model → Prediction → Action — here's how that applies to my project..."

## Guided Activity
In pairs, students each give a 1-minute practice presentation of their chosen project to their partner, using the 3-point structure above. Partners give one piece of friendly feedback.

## Main Activity / Project
Each student (or pair) presents their chosen AI project to the class (or, if smaller group/home setting, to family): demo it live, explain the AI stand-in, and describe what a real AI upgrade would add.

## Fun Challenge
⭐ **Bonus Challenge:** Answer one question from the audience about how their project's if/else or broadcast logic works.

## Check for Understanding
- Can the student clearly state which part of their finished project is real, working Scratch logic, and which part is a stand-in for future AI?
- Can they explain the 5-step AI pipeline using their own project's language?

## Common Student Mistakes
- Rushing through the demo without explaining WHY something happens.
- Forgetting to mention what part is a stand-in versus real AI (important for honesty about their own project!).
- Being too quiet — encourage clear, confident voices; practice runs help a lot.

## Differentiation
- **Extra support:** Allow presenting with a written note card of the 3 points; presentations can be to just the teacher or one peer instead of the whole group.
- **Extra challenge:** Present a project that has ALSO been upgraded with a real trained AI model (webcam/mic) following the integration guides, and explain the extra steps that took.

## Recap
🏆 **Victory Moment:** Congratulate every student — across this course, they learned what AI really is, trained two real machine learning models (image and sound), connected AI into Scratch, and built three complete, playable AI-ready projects!

## Take-Home / Creative Challenge
Show your presentation to a family member at home and ask them one question about how AI models learn from examples — see if you can answer it!

## Teacher Talking Points
- "You just did something real AI engineers do every day: build the logic, document what's real vs. what's next, and explain it clearly to others."
- "Whichever project you build on next — keep exploring, keep training models, and keep being curious about how machines learn!"
