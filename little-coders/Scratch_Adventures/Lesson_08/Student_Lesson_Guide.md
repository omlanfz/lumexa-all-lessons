# 🚀 Lesson 8: Final Project — Build Your Own Game!

## Hi, coder! 👋

This is it — the moment you've been building toward this whole course. Today, you get to design and build your very own real, playable Scratch game!

## 🧠 Think Like a Coder: Your Coding Toolbox

You've collected SO many tools. Let's remember them all:
- 🔵 **Motion** — move your character
- 🟣 **Looks** — change appearance, say things
- 🟪 **Sound** — add effects and music
- 🟡 **Events** — react to key presses, clicks, the green flag
- 🟠 **Broadcasting** — send messages between sprites
- 🔁 **Loops** — repeat actions
- 💎 **Conditionals** — make decisions
- 🧮 **Variables** — remember a Score or a count

A game is really just a story that keeps checking: did the player win yet? That's loops + conditionals + a Score variable, all working together!

## 📝 Plan Your Game First

Before you build, answer these questions:
1. **My game's goal:** What is the player trying to do?
2. **Controls:** What keys or clicks does the player use?
3. **Win condition:** What happens when they succeed?

## Try This: A Simple Click-and-Score Game

1. Create a variable called **Score**. Add **"when green flag clicked"** → **"set Score to 0."**
2. Add **"when this sprite clicked"** → **"change Score by 1"** → **"play sound until done."**
3. Add a separate **"forever"** loop with **"if <Score = 5> then"** → **"say [You win!] for 2 seconds"** → **"stop [all]."**
4. Click the green flag, then click your sprite 5 times — do you win?

## 🎮 Game Challenge: Build YOUR Game

Now use your plan to build your own game! Make sure you include:
- At least one sprite
- At least one sound
- At least one loop
- At least one conditional
- At least one variable (like Score)

Test often — click the green flag after every few blocks, not just at the very end!

## ⭐ Bonus Challenge

Add a win sound effect, a second sprite (a friend or an obstacle), or a backdrop that matches your game's theme. Extra advanced: add a "Lives" variable and a losing condition too!

## 🔍 Debug Detective

Uh-oh! Someone's Score variable never goes up, even though they keep clicking their sprite. What could be wrong? (Hint: is the "change Score by 1" block actually connected to a "when this sprite clicked" hat block? Is the Score variable's checkbox turned on so you can even see it?)
