# Course 10 — Scratch Adventures

**Ages 6–11 · 8 Lessons · 3 Portfolio Projects**

The perfect first step for young learners. Scratch uses visual blocks instead of text, so your child focuses on logic and creativity, not syntax. By the end, they have real animated projects to show off.

## Why It Matters

Every programmer starts with the same big ideas: sequence, events, loops, and decisions. Scratch teaches these ideas visually, so young learners build real logical thinking before they ever type a line of code.

## Lesson Breakdown

1. What is coding and why does it matter
2. Scratch setup and first sprite
3. Motion, looks, and sounds blocks
4. Events and broadcasting
5. Loops and repetition
6. Conditionals and decisions
7. Building an interactive story
8. Final project: our own game

## Portfolio Projects

1. **Animated Animal Story** — "Pip the Bunny's Big Adventure": a 3-scene animated story with hopping animation, sound, and broadcast-driven scene changes.
2. **Interactive Quiz Game** — "Quizzy's Space Quiz": a real 5-question ask-and-wait quiz with scorekeeping and a celebratory results screen.
3. **Creative Music Maker** — "Lumexa Sound Studio": 5 clickable instruments with pitched tones and pulsing visual feedback, so kids can compose by clicking around.

Each ships as a real, complete, playable `project.sb3` file — open it directly in the Scratch editor.
