# 🚀 Lesson 2: Meet Scratch and Your First Sprite

## Hi again, coder! 👋

Remember when you gave clear step-by-step instructions with no computer at all? Today you get to do that for real, inside a coding tool called **Scratch**!

## 🧠 Think Like a Coder: What's in Scratch?

When you open Scratch, you'll see four important areas:
- **The Stage** — like a little TV screen where your story or game happens.
- **The Sprite List** — shows all the characters in your project.
- **The Block Palette** — colorful blocks, each one an instruction, sorted by color and type.
- **The Code Area** — the empty space where you snap blocks together.

## 🤖 Robot Tip

Every Scratch block is one clear instruction — just like when you told your partner "take 3 steps forward" in the Human Robot game! When you snap blocks together, you're building a stack of instructions your sprite will follow, one after another.

## Your First Steps

1. Open Scratch and look at the Cat sprite (it's already there for you).
2. Find the **Motion** blocks (they're blue). Drag a **"move 10 steps"** block into the code area.
3. Click directly on the block. Watch — the cat moves!
4. Find the **Looks** blocks (purple). Drag a **"say Hello for 2 seconds"** block and snap it UNDER your move block.
5. Click the whole stack. Your cat moves AND says hello!

## 🎨 Make It Yours: Add a New Sprite

1. Click the little sprite button near the bottom right (it looks like a cat face with a plus sign).
2. Pick any sprite you like from the library — an animal, food, a fantasy creature, anything!
3. Give your new sprite a fun name.
4. Add a "move" block and a "say" block that introduces your sprite, like: "Hi, I'm Sparkle the Unicorn!"
5. Click the green flag at the top of the stage to run your whole project.

## ⭐ Bonus Challenge

Can you make your sprite move, say something, change its costume, AND make a sound — all in one stack of blocks? Try adding a "next costume" block and a sound block to your stack!

## 🔍 Debug Detective

Uh-oh! Someone's cat sprite has a "move 10 steps" block in the code area, but nothing happens when they click it. What could be wrong?
- Did they click directly ON the block?
- Is the Cat sprite actually selected in the sprite list?
- Is the sprite maybe off the edge of the stage where you can't see it move?

Try checking these clues like a real debug detective!
