# Lesson 2: Blast Off Into Scratch — Setup and Your First Sprite 🚀

## Big Idea
Scratch is our coding playground, and a sprite is a character we bring to life by snapping together colorful blocks like puzzle pieces.

## Learning Objectives
- Open the Scratch editor and identify its main areas (stage, block palette, code area, sprite list).
- Understand what a "sprite" and a "block" are.
- Drag and snap at least one block into the code area and run it by clicking it.
- Add a new sprite from the sprite library and give it a costume.
- Save their project (or understand how the teacher will save it for them).

## Teacher Preparation
- Ensure every device can reach the Scratch editor (https://scratch.mit.edu/ — "Create" button, no login required to build, though an account helps to save online).
- Test that clicking a block plays a sound/does an action, and that the green flag works, on your classroom devices beforehand.
- Have a simple "my first project" example ready to show onscreen.
- Remind students of Lesson 1: "Remember how WE had to give super clear instructions to our robot? Today, Scratch blocks ARE those instructions — but this time, snapped together like puzzle pieces!"

## Teaching Flow (approx. 45 minutes)
1. Warm-up / Hook — 4 min
2. Concept Introduction — 8 min
3. Teacher Demonstration — 8 min
4. Guided Coding — 10 min
5. Mini Challenge — 5 min
6. Main Activity — 8 min
7. Check for Understanding + Recap — 2 min

## Warm-up / Hook (4 min)
🚀 **Coding Mission:** "Remember last time, we became robots and coders without a computer? Today we get to open a real coding tool called Scratch, where our instructions become blocks we can literally snap together, like Lego bricks — and today, YOU get to bring a character to life for the first time!"

## Concept Introduction (8 min)
- **The Stage** — like a little TV screen where our story or game happens.
- **Sprites** — the characters or objects in our project (a cat, a dog, a rocket ship — anything!). Explain: "A sprite is like an actor. It can move, talk, and change costumes, but only if we give it code."
- **Blocks** — colorful puzzle pieces, each one a single instruction (like "move," "say," or "play sound"). Different colors mean different types of instructions (motion blocks are blue, looks blocks are purple, sound blocks are pink/magenta).
- **The Code Area** — the empty space where we snap blocks together to build a script (a stack of instructions).
- 🤖 **Robot Tip:** "Remember our robot game? Each Scratch block is one clear instruction — just like 'take 3 steps forward.' When you snap blocks together, you're writing a recipe the sprite will follow exactly."

## Teacher Demonstration (8 min)
1. Open Scratch, point out: Stage (top left/center), Sprite list (bottom), Block Palette (middle), Code Area (right).
2. Click on the built-in Cat sprite. Say: "This is our very first sprite!"
3. Drag a "motion_movesteps" block into the code area.
4. Click directly on the block — show that the cat moves! Say: "See that? Clicking a block runs it immediately, just like a test button."
5. Snap a "looks_say" block underneath, type "Hello, I'm a sprite!" Click the stack — show the cat says it.
6. Show how to add a NEW sprite: click the sprite button (bottom right, cat-face icon with a plus), choose one from the library.
7. Show costumes tab briefly: "Look — this sprite even has different outfits called costumes!"

## Guided Coding (10 min)
Students, on their own devices (or in pairs), open Scratch and:
1. Find and click on the default Cat sprite.
2. Drag a "move 10 steps" block into the code area.
3. Click it a few times and watch the cat move across the stage.
4. Add a "say Hello for 2 seconds" block underneath it, and click the stack to run it.

## Mini Challenge (5 min)
Add ONE more block of your choice (any color!) underneath your stack, and figure out — just by clicking it and watching — what it does. Be ready to explain it to a partner in your own words.

## Main Activity / Project (8 min)
**Add and Meet a New Sprite:** Students click the "add sprite" button and choose a brand-new sprite from the library (an animal, a food, a fantasy character — anything they like). They:
1. Rename their sprite (click the name field in the sprite info area, type a fun new name).
2. Give it a "move" block and a "say" block with a message that introduces the sprite (e.g., "Hi, I'm Sparkle the Unicorn!").
3. Click the green flag to test their whole stack together.

## Fun Challenge (optional, harder) ⭐
Add a second costume change to your sprite's script using a "next costume" block, and try adding a sound block too, so your sprite moves, talks, changes look, AND makes a sound — all from clicking one stack!

## Check for Understanding
- "What is a sprite?"
- "What is a block, and what happens when you click on it by itself?"
- "Where on the screen do you see the sprite actually move or talk?"
- "How do you add a brand-new sprite to your project?"

## Common Student Mistakes (with fixes)
- **Mistake:** Blocks don't snap together (there's a gap). **Fix:** Show them to drag the new block so its top notch is right under the bump of the block above — it will highlight with a white outline when it's close enough to snap.
- **Mistake:** Clicking the block palette instead of dragging. **Fix:** Remind them: click AND HOLD, then drag into the code area, then let go.
- **Mistake:** Forgetting which sprite they're coding for (blocks appear to "not work"). **Fix:** Check the sprite list — whichever sprite is highlighted/selected is the one being coded right now.
- 🔍 **Debug Detective:** "Uh-oh! A student's cat sprite isn't moving when clicked, even though there's a 'move 10 steps' block in the code area. What's wrong?" (Answer: the block isn't actually clicked/selected sprite might be different, or the block isn't connected to anything and needs to be clicked directly, or the sprite is off-stage. Walk through checking: Is the right sprite selected? Is the block by itself clickable? Try clicking it directly.)

## Differentiation
- **Struggling support:** Pair them with a partner; focus only on move + say blocks; teacher pre-selects the new sprite from the library for them.
- **Standard:** Follow full lesson as planned.
- **Extension for advanced learners:** Have them add TWO sprites, each with its own move+say script, and predict out loud what will happen when they click the green flag for the whole project (both sprites act at once!).

## Recap
"Today you opened Scratch for the very first time, met your first sprite, and code by snapping blocks together like puzzle pieces. You made a sprite move, talk, and even added a brand-new character of your own!"

## Take-Home / Creative Challenge
Think of a sprite you'd love to bring to life next time (a dragon, a soccer ball, your pet) — sketch what it might look like and one silly thing you'd want it to say.

## Teacher Talking Points
- "Look at that — you just wrote your very first line of real code, and it worked!"
- "It's totally okay if a block doesn't do what you expected. That's not 'breaking' anything — that's exploring!"
- "Every single block is like one instruction from our robot game — you already know how this works."
- "I can already tell some amazing sprites are about to come to life in this room."

## 🏆 Victory Moment
Have every student click their green flag at the same time so the whole room's sprites move and talk together. Celebrate: "You just ran real code, together, as a whole class of coders!"
